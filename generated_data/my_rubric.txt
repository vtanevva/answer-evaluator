Inflation is the sustained rise in the general level of prices of goods and services over time, leading to a fall in the purchasing power of money. When prices increase, each unit of currency buys fewer goods and services than before. Inflation is usually measured by the percentage change in a price index such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI).
Moderate inflation is common in growing economies, as it encourages spending and investment. However, high or unpredictable inflation can harm economic stability, reducing savings and discouraging investment. Causes of inflation can include demand-pull factors—when total demand in an economy rises faster than total supply—and cost-push factors, such as increases in wages or the price of raw materials. Governments and central banks try to control inflation through monetary policy, for example, by adjusting interest rates to influence spending and borrowing.
